# Further Reading, Mapped to the Book

Each modern work from [`further-reading.md`](further-reading.md), placed in the book chapter it
supports. Chapter numbers follow the table of contents on the `book` branch. Talks and podcasts
are listed only where they are **by the same author, on the same subject**, and the link was
found and checked.

† Video found through search. YouTube rate-limited the automated link check, so open it once to
confirm.

---

## Preface: The Line I Won't Help You Cross

- **Bok, *Lying* (1978).** The most thorough modern treatment of when deception is ever justified. It backs the ethical floor.
- **Hadnagy & Schulman, *Human Hacking* (2021).** Social engineering skills turned toward everyday life.
  ⚑ DEF CON banned Hadnagy in 2022 over code-of-conduct reports. His lawsuit against DEF CON was dismissed with prejudice in 2025. The book now leans harder on elicitation, which is his core topic, so decide how to handle him before print.
  **Media:** [Negotiations Ninja ep. 391, "Chris Hadnagy's 7 Principles of Elicitation"](https://negotiations.ninja/podcast/chris-hadnagys-7-principles-of-elicitation-ep-391/)

## Part One: Authority

### Ch 3: The Real Call Graph
- **DeMarco & Lister, *Peopleware* (1987; 3rd ed. 2013).** "The major problems of our work are not so much technological as sociological." It's the software-world version of Trist & Bamforth.
- **Meadows, *Thinking in Systems* (2008).** The general systems vocabulary.
- **Krackhardt & Hanson, "Informal Networks" (*HBR* 1993).** The informal organization, mapped in practice.
- **Allen, *Managing the Flow of Technology* (1977).** How information actually moves inside engineering organizations; the "Allen curve."
- **Burt, *Structural Holes* (1992).** Advantage goes to people who bridge separate groups. It extends Granovetter.
- **Cross & Parker, *The Hidden Power of Social Networks* (2004).** Network analysis applied inside companies.
- **Covey, *The Speed of Trust* (2006).** Trust as speed and cost, the popular form of Arrow.
- **Skelton & Pais, *Team Topologies* (2019).** Conway's Law put to work, including the inverse Conway maneuver.

### Ch 4: Go and Do Likewise
- **Cohen & Bradford, *Influence Without Authority* (3rd ed. 2017).** The standard text on lateral influence.
- **Reilly, "Being Glue" (2019).** Gives "making work legible" its positive meaning, and names the career cost.
  **Media:** ["Technical leadership and glue work," LeadDev NY 2019](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KClAPipnKqw)
- **Reilly, *The Staff Engineer's Path* (2022).** Leading without a title in engineering.
- **Forsgren, Humble & Kim, *Accelerate* (2018).** Metrics as the SRE's authority: facts that people accept.
- **Ibarra, *Act Like a Leader, Think Like a Leader* (2015).** Act your way into leadership; "outsight."
  **Media:** [2017 HR Congress keynote](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4pk9TkHRZmI) · [conversation videos](https://herminiaibarra.com/video-conversations/)
- **Kotter, "Leading Change" (*HBR* 1995).** The guiding coalition, the modern form of "build the coalition before the meeting."
- **Liker, *The Toyota Way* (2004).** *Nemawashi*, building consensus before the decision.
- **Allen, T. D., et al., mentoring meta-analysis (2004).** Evidence that mentoring pays, for "mentoring up."

## Part Two: Competency

### Ch 5: The Rifle in the Vise
- **Brown, *Rising Strong* (2015).** "The story I'm telling myself," which is assuming good intent turned into a practice.
  **Media:** [TED, "The power of vulnerability"](https://www.ted.com/talks/brene_brown_the_power_of_vulnerability). This one is on adjacent ground rather than the book's exact subject.

### Ch 6: Promoted and Abandoned
- **Ross, "The Intuitive Psychologist" (1977).** Names the fundamental attribution error. Promote it to origin status, since the book now leans on it.
- **Fournier, *The Manager's Path* (2017).** What the newly promoted engineering manager is actually facing. It's useful for empathy.

### Ch 8: Saving Face
- **Rock, "SCARF" (2008).** Status, certainty, autonomy, relatedness and fairness as social threats. A modern framing of face.
- **Patterson et al., *Crucial Conversations* (2002).** Keeping high-stakes conversations safe.
- **Scott, *Radical Candor* (2017).** Challenging directly while caring personally: feedback without blame.
  **Media:** [Radical Candor podcast](https://www.radicalcandor.com/podcast) · [YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/@RadicalCandor)
- **Pfeffer, *Power* (2010).** Status and power, unvarnished. It's a useful counterweight to the book's optimism. (Also Ch 10.)
  **Media:** [Interview on *Power*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5cxZb8gXHzo)

### Ch 9: Elicitation
- **Huang et al., "It Doesn't Hurt to Ask" (2017).** Asking questions, especially follow-ups, increases liking. It's the closest modern evidence that elicitation builds the relationship rather than straining it.
- **Voss & Raz, *Never Split the Difference* (2016).** Calibrated "how" and "what" questions, and labeling. Elicitation under pressure.
  **Media:** [Crisp podcast ep. 5, Chris Voss](https://crisp.co/podcast/episode-5-chris-voss/)
- **Locke & Latham, "Goal Setting" (2002).** Thirty-five years of evidence for specificity.
- **Gabarro & Kotter, "Managing Your Boss" (*HBR* 1980).** The classic text on managing up.
- **Conger, "The Necessary Art of Persuasion" (*HBR* 1998).** Framing in the other person's terms.
- **Ury, *Getting Past No* (1991).** "Go to the balcony," a long-form version of "I'll get back to you."
- **Loftus, "Planting Misinformation" (2005).** Memory is rewritten after the fact, so write the recap.
  **Media:** [TED, "How reliable is your memory?"](https://www.ted.com/talks/elizabeth_loftus_how_reliable_is_your_memory)
- **Columbia Accident Investigation Board, *Report, Vol. I* (2003).** What bad news delivered late costs, in the extreme.
- **Burris, "The Risks and Rewards of Speaking Up" (2012).** How managers actually respond to employee voice. It sets realistic expectations.
- **Cain, *Quiet* (2012).** Being heard without being loud.
  **Media:** [TED, "The power of introverts"](https://www.ted.com/talks/susan_cain_the_power_of_introverts)
- **Heath & Heath, *Made to Stick* (2007).** Stories in the room.
- **Small, Loewenstein & Slovic, "Sympathy and Callousness" (2007).** One story outweighs a statistic, so narrative wins in the room and data wins in the follow-up.

## Part Three: Peace

### Ch 10: One Strategy for Every Manager
- **Grant, *Give and Take* (2013).** Otherish versus selfless giving, and why the record and the end date matter.
  **Media:** [TED, "Are you a giver or a taker?"](https://www.ted.com/talks/adam_grant_are_you_a_giver_or_a_taker) · [*WorkLife* and *ReThinking* podcasts](https://adamgrant.net/podcasts/)
- **Hewlett, *Forget a Mentor, Find a Sponsor* (2013).** "Mentors advise. Sponsors act." Sponsors as part of the wider network.
  **Media:** [*Forget a Mentor, Find a Sponsor* talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i83HLA3a0DQ) †
- **Ibarra, Carter & Silva, "Why Men Still Get More Promotions Than Women" (*HBR* 2010).** Who gets sponsored, and who doesn't.
- **Evans, "Get your work recognized: write a brag document" (2019).** The record, kept for yourself. It's your parachute.
- **Pfeffer, *Power*.** See Ch 8.

### Ch 11: Ambush the Ambiguity
- **Cialdini, *Influence*, New and Expanded (2021).** Adds Unity as a seventh principle.
  **Media:** see Cialdini in `source-excerpts.md`.
- **Stajano & Wilson, "Understanding Scam Victims" (2011).** Seven principles of how scams work. It backs the bridge from a struggling manager to the scammer's conditions.
- **Ferreira, Coventry & Lenzini, "Principles of Persuasion… in Phishing" (2015).** Cialdini's principles mapped onto phishing.
- **Langer, Blank & Chanowitz, "Mindlessness" (1978).** People comply with any request that has a "because" attached. That mindless compliance is the attack surface.
- **Verizon, *2024 DBIR*.** How much of real breach activity involves the human element.
- **Maslach & Leiter, "Understanding the Burnout Experience" (2016).** Burnout's modern model, including the job conditions that drive it.
- **WHO, "Burn-out an 'occupational phenomenon'" (2019).** Burnout officially classed as a workplace phenomenon, not a medical condition.
- **Hadnagy, *Social Engineering* (2nd ed. 2018).** ⚑ See the Preface note.

### Ch 12: The Fake Boss
- **Chen & Magramo, CNN (Feb 2024).** The $25 million deepfake-CFO video call at Arup.
- **Nightingale & Farid, "AI-Synthesized Faces…" (2022).** People rate AI faces as *more* trustworthy than real ones, which is how "polished means competent" fails.
- **NSA, FBI & CISA, *Contextualizing Deepfake Threats to Organizations* (2023).** Official guidance, including out-of-band verification.

## Part Four: Joy

### Ch 13: Prompting Is Managing
- **Dell'Acqua et al., "Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier" (2023).** Consultants using AI did better on tasks inside its capabilities and *worse* on tasks outside them. Judgment about where the frontier lies is the manager's job.
  **Media:** [Ethan Mollick (co-author) on the study](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dPJ6Bxsky0s) †
- **Parasuraman & Manzey, "Complacency and Bias…" (2010).** Over-trusting automation, a modern Bainbridge.
- **Agrawal, Gans & Goldfarb, *Prediction Machines* (2018).** When prediction gets cheap, judgment becomes valuable.
- **Salvi et al., "Conversational Persuasiveness of LLMs" (2024).** LLMs persuade at least as well as people, more so with personal data. The flip side of the sparring partner.
- **Hazell, "Spear Phishing With LLMs" (2023).** The same capability aimed at you. (Also Ch 12.)
- **Microsoft, *Work Trend Index 2023*.** Attention overload at work, and the case being made for AI to relieve it.

### Ch 14: Week One
- **Fogg, *Tiny Habits* (2019).** Shrink the behavior, anchor it to a routine, and celebrate. It's "one question on Monday" as a method.
  **Media:** ["Forget big change, start with a tiny habit," TEDxFremont](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AdKUJxjn-R8) †
- **Amabile & Kramer, *The Progress Principle* (2011).** Small wins drive motivation at work.
- **Fiske, Cuddy & Glick, "Warmth and Competence" (2007).** People judge others first on warmth and then on competence. That's the modern version of Aristotle's three stats.
  **Media:** Cuddy's [TED talk](https://www.ted.com/talks/amy_cuddy_your_body_language_may_shape_who_you_are) is on power posing, a different topic whose findings have replication problems. Don't cite it here.
- **Antonakis, Fenley & Liechti, "Learning Charisma" (*HBR* 2012).** The practical version of their 2011 study.
- **Cabane, *The Charisma Myth* (2012).** Charisma as presence, power and warmth that can be learned.
