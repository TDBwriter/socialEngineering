# Where to Read the Origin Sources

Digital locations for the origin sources in [`bibliography.md`](bibliography.md), with
priority given to the oldest — the first statements of each idea. Beat numbers refer to
[`outline.md`](outline.md).

**Every URL below returned HTTP 200 on 2026-10-03.** Access tiers were read from each
item's catalog record, not guessed. Archive.org changes access modes over time, so a
borrow-only item may open up (or close) later.

## What checking the sources settled

Four citation questions resolved against actual texts and catalog records, not memory:

1. **Aristotle II.1 — confirmed, verbatim.** The *Rhetoric* II.1 passage reads: *"There are
   three things which inspire confidence in the orator's own character—the three, namely,
   that induce us to believe a thing apart from any proof of it: good sense, good moral
   character, and goodwill."* Beat 49's "three stats to raise" rests on exactly this.
   The bibliography's justification is accurate as written.

2. **Kant Appendix II — confirmed.** The Gutenberg table of contents gives the full title:
   *"Appendix II — Concerning the Harmony of Politics with Morals According to the
   Transcendental Idea of Public Right."* Beats 5 and 51 are pointing at the right place.

3. **Wiener's publisher — corrected, now with a catalog record behind it.** The archive.org
   entry for the 1948 edition catalogues it as **"New York, J. Wiley."** The bibliography's
   "MIT Press/Wiley, 1948" conflates the 1948 Wiley first edition with the MIT Press
   second edition of 1961. Cite one or the other.

4. **Shannon's two parts — confirmed.** *BSTJ* 27(3), July 1948, pp. 379–423 **and** 27(4),
   October 1948, pp. 623–656. Citing 27(3) alone covers half the paper.

Two that did **not** resolve:

- **Conway's volume and issue.** Conway's own site gives only "*Datamation*, April 1968."
  Secondary sources split between 14(4) and 14(5). Safest citation is month and year plus
  the author's page; don't assert an issue number.
- **Cialdini's 1984 first edition.** The bibliography cites William Morrow, 1984, and the
  "why it's the origin" column rests on that first statement of the six principles.
  Archive.org has the 1993 printing and later; the 1984 first edition is not digitised.
  The principles were reworded across editions, so a quotation taken from the 1993 copy
  should not be attributed to 1984 without checking print.

Also worth noting: **Goffman's 1956 Edinburgh monograph** — cited in the bibliography
alongside the 1959 Anchor edition — does not appear to exist digitally anywhere. Only
the 1959 Doubleday text is available.
