# Format: 60-Minute Presentation

Status: **format of record.** Content is the full 52 beats in [`outline.md`](../outline.md).
This file holds only the timing plan, which is a derived proposal and not yet the author's.

---

## The timing problem, stated plainly

52 beats in a 60-minute slot. A 60-minute conference slot is realistically **50 minutes of
content plus 8–10 minutes of Q&A**, which gives you:

> **50 min ÷ 52 beats ≈ 58 seconds per beat.**

That is a hard constraint, not a rounding error. At 58 seconds a beat you can state the
claim, name the source, and give one line of support. You cannot tell a story. The talk
as outlined is therefore a *dense aphoristic* talk, and it will work only if delivered
that way deliberately.

Three ways out, in order of how much they cost:

1. **Keep all 52 and commit to the form.** Tight, quotable, high-tempo. The citation
   discipline becomes the engine: each beat is claim + attribution + one line. Risk: the
   audience gets no room to breathe and retains the vibe rather than the content.
2. **Promote 6–8 beats to "full" treatment (2–3 min each with a story) and demote the
   rest to rapid-fire runs of 20–30 seconds.** This is the usual fix and probably the
   right one. Candidates for full treatment are marked ★ below.
3. **Cut an act.** Act VII (AI) is the most separable; Act V (managing up) is the most
   separable from the title. Cutting either buys roughly 6–7 minutes.

## Beats proposed for full treatment (★)

Each gets 2–3 minutes with a concrete story; everything else in its act runs fast to pay for it.

- **★ 1** — the premise. Needs a real debugging-a-social-system anecdote or the talk has no ground.
- **★ 8** — Conway's Law in both directions. The reversal is the most surprising claim in Act II.
- **★ 16** — specificity is charisma. The most immediately actionable beat in the talk.
- **★ 25** — the meeting is a ratification ceremony. Pairs with Asch; high recognition value.
- **★ 35** — urgency, authority, isolation. The spine of Act VI; everything else in that act hangs off it.
- **★ 45** — "person who vouches for this." The sharpest line in Act VII.
- **★ 51** — the ethical floor. Never rush this one.
- **★ 52** — the close.

## Delivery notes carried from the source

- On beats 12, 28, 6, 44 and 5, say "borrowing from Shannon / Wiener / Turing / Kant"
  rather than "Shannon showed." These are analogies, not evidence, and a technical
  audience will notice the difference. (See the notes in [`bibliography.md`](../bibliography.md).)
- Beat 3 contains a deliberate conflict: Weber held that charisma could not be taught,
  Antonakis tested the opposite. Stage it as old view versus evidence rather than hiding it.
