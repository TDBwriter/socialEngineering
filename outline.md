## Abstract

Engineers learn to read technical systems but rarely the social systems around them. This talk argues that charisma is a learnable skill, a "build," and treats social engineering as a neutral set of influence techniques that engineers can use to lead and also need to defend against. It draws on sociotechnical systems theory, organizational sociology, persuasion research, and security practice. The talk covers how influence moves through an enterprise, how to lead peers without formal authority, how to manage the relationship with a manager, and how to spot manipulation aimed at you. It then looks at how generative AI raises the stakes on both sides: AI makes polished output cheap, so judgment, trust, and verification become worth more. The talk ends with a practical plan for building these skills and a firm ethical limit: if a technique works only when the other person doesn't know you're using it, don't use it. Wherever possible, each claim is traced to the person who first stated the idea.
