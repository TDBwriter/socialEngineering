# Format: Book (Expansion)

Status: **proposal, not the author's plan.** Beat numbers refer to [`outline.md`](../outline.md).

---

## Sizing

52 beats. If each beat expands to a section of 800–1,200 words, the body lands at
**42,000–62,000 words** — a normal trade non-fiction length — before front matter,
case material and exercises. The outline is therefore already close to book-shaped
in volume. What it lacks is not length but *evidence of the second kind*: stories.

## The one structural change the book needs

**The ethical floor has to move to the front.**

In the talk, beat 5 pre-commits the ethics and beat 51 pays it off 46 slides later.
That works because a talk is linear and the audience cannot skip ahead. A book is
browsable: a reader can open directly to the self-defense chapter or to "the six
primitives, and where each fails at scale." A book that withholds its ethical frame
until Chapter 9 is, to a browsing reader, a manipulation manual with an apology at
the back.

So: beat 51 becomes part of the frame in Chapter 1 and recurs as a standing test at
the end of each chapter, and beat 5 becomes the preface. Beat 51's current position
in Act VIII survives as the restatement, not the reveal.

## Why Act VII should be distributed, not quarantined

Act VII (beats 42–48) is the most time-sensitive material in the project. As a standalone
chapter it will be the first thing to date, and it will read as bolted on — which is the
standard failure mode of an AI chapter in a book that is not about AI.

Distributed instead:

| Beat | Goes to | Because |
|---|---|---|
| 42 | Ch. 3 | It is a claim about attention economics; it belongs next to Simon (beat 13), which it already shares a source with. |
| 43 | Ch. 1 | "Judgment and trust are the scarce goods" is a premise, not a postscript. It strengthens the book's reason to exist. |
| 44 | Ch. 7 | Broken heuristics is a self-defense claim. |
| 45 | Ch. 8 | "Person who vouches for this" is a description of the build's end state. |
| 46 | Ch. 7 | Persuasion amplifier aimed at you. |
| 47 | Ch. 7 | Out-of-band verification is the countermeasure to 44 and 46. |
| 48 | Ch. 1 or Afterword | The thesis-level claim about what survives. |

Each chapter then carries its own "what AI changes here" section, which ages in place
instead of all at once.

## The expansion task, named

The outline is **52 assertions and zero anecdotes.** A talk can run on assertion plus
delivery; a book cannot. The real work of the expansion is not writing more words per
beat — it is finding, for each beat, one of:

1. a first-person story from the author's own enterprise experience,
2. a documented case (the Columbia report on beat 29 and the CNN deepfake story on
   beat 44 are already in [`further-reading.md`](../further-reading.md) and are exactly this), or
3. a worked example the reader can check against their own org.

**Suggested next action:** go through beats 1–52 and mark each one A (has a story), B
(knows where to find one), or C (has nothing). The C list is the book's actual scope.

## Carried-over obligations

- The citation discipline — "each claim traced to the person who first stated the idea" —
  is a stronger promise in a book than in a talk, and reviewers will check it. The
  verification pass in [`NOTES.md`](../NOTES.md) stops being optional.
- The analogy/evidence distinction (Shannon, Wiener, Turing, Kant) must be explicit in
  prose. In a talk it is a word choice; in print it is a claim about what has been proven.
- The gaming metaphor (build, spec, stats, respec — beats 3 and 49) currently appears
  twice in 52 beats and carries the title. In a book it either becomes a sustained
  organizing device or it should be dropped from the title. Half-committed metaphors
  read worse at length than at speed.
